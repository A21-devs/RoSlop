# Modeling pets, bosses, eggs and weapons

Players buy, hatch and show these off, so they have to look great in a thumbnail and from across a server. Aim for the top simulators and tycoons on the charts right now: cute and readable, bright, a clear tier ladder, and every item recognisable from its silhouette alone.

## Pets

- Proportions sell cute. The head is half the pet or more. Eyes are big and set low on the face, with a bright highlight dot. Bodies are short and round, legs are stubby, and nothing is thin or spiky unless it's the point of the pet.
- One idea per pet (a lava dino, a crystal bunny, a pirate cat) that reads in a second. Variants of a base pet change colour, material and one accessory, not the whole body.
- Keep them low poly: about 500 to 2,500 triangles. Bevel the edges. Smooth shading with a hand-painted texture or clean gradients, never default grey.
- Rig with a few bones (body, head, tail, ears or wings) for bobbing, hopping and a hatch pop. Pets float or bounce beside the player, so the pivot goes at the bottom centre.

## Eggs

- Eggs are the slot machine. The shape is the same across the set, so the paint does the talking: pattern, colours and a material hint at what's inside (cracked lava, mossy stone, frosty, galaxy).
- Higher tiers get glow, sparkle particles and a pedestal, and look worth the price from far away.

## Weapons

- Oversized and readable. A sword is 4 to 6 studs, much bigger than real. The silhouette alone should tell a common blade from a legendary one.
- The tier ladder is shape plus colour plus effects. Common: plain shape, muted colours. Rare: an extra flourish and brighter colours. Epic: an unusual silhouette and emissive lines. Legendary and mythic: a dramatic shape, glowing parts, trails and particles.
- Put the grip where the hand goes, with the Handle at the grip. Keep a weapon under about 3,000 triangles, and texture it per the texturing guide (bright edge highlights on metal, wrapped leather grips, gems that glow from inside).

## Bosses

- Huge and unmistakable: 3 to 8 times the player's height, with one strong silhouette (horns, a shell, wings, a giant weapon) and a colour that pops against the arena.
- Make weak points and attack wind-ups visible: glowing eyes, cores, or a raised arm before the slam. Put glow and particles on the parts that matter.
- Rig it for big readable animations (idle breathing, wind-up, slam, stagger, death). Keep the hitbox simple and separate from the detailed mesh.

## Rarity colours (keep the same everywhere: items, UI, eggs, nameplates)

Common grey, Uncommon green, Rare blue, Epic purple, Legendary gold or orange, Mythic red or pink, Secret black with a rainbow edge. Match the game's own ladder if it already has one.

## Building simulators and tycoons from parts

Part-built maps like the top simulators and tycoons look clean, not cheap:

- Smooth plastic or soft materials in bright, saturated, friendly colours. Use 2 or 3 tones of each colour for lit, mid and shadow faces instead of one flat colour.
- Chunky shapes with rounded corners (cylinders and balls at the corners, bevelled wedges on edges), and thick outlines made of slightly bigger dark parts where the style calls for it.
- Bases and plots are on a strict grid, with clear borders, numbered or named signs, and the upgrade path visible as you walk in.
- Zones are colour coded and themed (grass, desert, snow, lava, space), with a big arch or portal at each entrance showing the price or level to get in.
- Keep the play space open and flat where players run, and put the detail around the edges.
